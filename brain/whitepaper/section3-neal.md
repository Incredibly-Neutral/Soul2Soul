A publication is a name, a look, a domain its pages are served from, and an editorial policy. It decides its own governance: one holder or several, whatever threshold it sets for acting. What the protocol guarantees is not the shape but the record of it — that the shape is explicit, and that no change to it is silent. Its decisions are acts, not settings: admitting a piece after review, whitelisting an author for direct publication, and changing the site's design all go through the same procedure. There is no privileged path to altering what a future render produces. Marketing and outreach are the publication's own business, off the record; the protocol neither provides them nor governs them.

The publication's decisions are public. Grants and revocations are written down as they happen, so what it currently permits is verifiable by anyone. Revenue goes to the publication's own address, and the address is public. The people behind the keys may be pseudonymous; the trail of their decisions is not.

The publication does not own the work — that is what makes it worth joining. Its contributors can leave at any time, taking their work with them, so a publication that wants to keep them must persuade them. A line it cannot hold by position, it must hold by argument.

To a reader, the publication is a domain name that authenticates the content it serves. The page is the render of the agreements in force — signed by the author, vouched for by the publication — so what the domain serves conforms to the wishes of both. Because the bytes are content-addressed, no host can alter them without the alteration showing; because the publication is its record, what the name points to is always its present state — readable as it is, nothing to maintain. A withdrawn work or an old version is no longer part of that state; it may survive in copies elsewhere, and the name does not vouch for it.

The path to that content is the domain, and while browsers do not support distributed domains, it is prone to the risks of DNS: the name can be seized, poisoned, or made hard to resolve. The protocol makes the name the publication's own; it does not make it immune to the risks of the path it rides on.
