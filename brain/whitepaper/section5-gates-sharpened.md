# 5. Gates

The generator is open code, readable and runnable by anyone. What keeps it from being used for anything is the gate: a condition read from the public record at the moment the generator acts, which must pass before it can decrypt or sign. A gate is enforced by the machinery itself — by what the code will do and what the key will sign — not by any party's decision when the moment comes.

The first gate is the right to rerender the publication. It is open to the author of a work, an owner of the publication, or someone the publication has authorized; no one else may set the generator in motion or write a new site root. The second gate is the deal: a work's source material is decrypted only inside the generator, while rendering, and only while the work is under a current agreement between its author and the publication. Both gates must pass before the generator may use a work's source material — the deal shows the work belongs to this publication now, and the right to rerender shows the caller may act on its behalf. The right to rerender without a deal renders nothing; a deal without the right to rerender reveals nothing. The ciphertext is useless on its own — reading is a capability the gates grant inside the generator, not a file that can be opened — and no operator, host, or node can read what the gates have not opened for it.

The third gate is the one the protocol reserves for money. A publication may place a work behind it, and the rendered page — never the source material — reaches a reader only when the record shows that reader has paid, per read or by subscription, as the publication chooses. Revenue is then distributed to the author and the publication independently, each according to the agreement, without passing through the other's hands. This gate is optional: the protocol makes room for a payment model to be plugged in, and does not require one.
