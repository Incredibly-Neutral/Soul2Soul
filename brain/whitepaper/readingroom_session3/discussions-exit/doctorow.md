# Cory Doctorow — discussion note: "departure costs both sides" (response to the authors' counter-argument)

## Where I land

This counter-argument does the thing my earlier note asked for: it finally gives the
publication's half of the ledger its own terms. The original draft sentence — "departure
costs both sides, because a publication that wants to keep its contributors has to persuade
them" — showed only the *mechanism* of the publication's cost (it must persuade) and never
the *loss*. Points 1 and 2 supply the loss. That is the fix, and it is the right fix. Most of
this is correct. Three things are over-claimed, one thing is still dodged, and point 5 needs
to be welded to the mechanism instead of floated above it. Here is where it goes, with
wording, and the one sentence I would add.

## What's right, and where it goes

The natural home is §1's final paragraph — "To a reader, a publication is still a name that
resolves..." — because that is where the load-bearing "departure costs both sides" claim
lives, and it should be expanded there, not parked in §3. §3's "The publication does not own
the work" paragraph can stay as the operational twin, and §4's withdrawal mechanics stay
as-is. But the claim is made in §1, so the ledger belongs in §1.

**Point 1** (the publication loses the work itself) is accurate to the protocol: the author
keeps ownership and revocation is immediate, so a departing author removes the render license
and the work leaves the publication's record — "un-vouched for," as the counter-argument
says. One precision: the work does not disappear from the world, only from the name's
vouching; the publication loses the render relationship. That is a real cost, and stating it
on its own terms was what was missing.

**Point 2** (the collective exit) is the strongest material, and it is what makes the ledger
honest rather than sentimental. Two of eight walking makes the name visibly not what it was —
and here is the part the counter-argument underuses: under this protocol that loss is *on the
record*. The chain shows the withdrawals; the name's own history testifies that it is
diminished. The identity loss is not felt, it is verifiable. That is a protocol-native way to
state point 2, and it converts "we'd miss them" into a structural fact. Keep it as the
dramatic case, though — most exits will not be identity-threatening, and the paper should not
drape every departure in the collective's robes.

**Point 3** (setup cost falls, competition rises) is squarely in my documented territory:
competition constrains extraction, and the reason incumbent platforms enshittify is precisely
that exit is expensive for the people inside and cheap for the platform. A field where any
author can find-or-found a home, and where the archive cannot be held hostage, is a real
discipline on publications. This is the complement to the bilateral exit cost and belongs in
the same breath.

**Point 6** (not for everyone) is right and consistent with the paper's best habit — the same
candor it applied to the state and to pseudonymity. Keep it.

## Over-claims to trim

1. Point 3's "That is a discipline no incumbent platform has ever faced." Incumbents face
   competition constantly; that is not the novelty. The novelty is the *mechanism*: exit is
   cheap for the author AND the archive cannot be held hostage, both at once. Say that.
   "No incumbent has ever faced" invites the reader to list counterexamples and win.
2. Point 5's adjectives — "more lively, more aspirational, more on-topic." That is vibes, not
   mechanism, and a skeptical reader pries at vibes. Keep the substance, cut the cheerleading.
3. Point 6's "there are more of them than the current media landscape serves." Unproven
   quantity. State it as premise or aspiration, not as fact.

## Point 4 and the sock-puppet question

Point 4 is necessary but it does not resolve my concern — it stops the dodge, and that is real
progress, but "case by case" is a placeholder, not an answer. Here is the distinction. My
sock-puppet worry is not about the empirical distribution of where audiences go; it is
structural: when a writer leaves, does the protocol give the writer a *mechanically real
channel* to the readers who want to follow? The draft's own sentence — "to a reader, a
publication is still a name that resolves" — locates the reader's relationship in the domain,
which the author cannot carry. If the author's identity, not the publication's domain, were
the addressable thing, then "case by case" would genuinely be up to readers. As it stands, the
counter-argument asserts "in many cases it follows the author" without the paper having built
the path that would make following mechanically possible. "The publication does not hold the
audience by default" (a negative) is not the same as "the author can hold the audience" (a
positive). The sock-puppet gap stands until the paper does one of two things: make the
byline/account the addressable identity a reader can follow (one sentence does it), or scope
reader exit out entirely with the same candor it applied to the state — admitting the protocol
does not solve sock-puppet exit, and that an author who walks without the audience has
repeated the platform problem under a smaller flag. "Case by case" is neither.

One wrinkle in the paper's favor: the collective case has a reader-side cost that cuts *with*
point 2. Readers who came for the *mix* lose the mix regardless of whom they follow. That
strengthens "departure costs both sides," but it is a third party's cost the paper should know
it is not counting.

(On warrants: the sock-puppet framing is my documented argument [Locus, Feb 2024]; its
application to this specific protocol is inference, as I flagged in my session-1 note. The
structural claim — that portability must be built, not hoped for — is doctrine; the "case by
case" analysis of this paper's design is mine.)

## Point 5: strength or concession?

Both, and the distinction matters. As a destination statement — "this is a climate of
association and dissociation at low friction" — it is a strength, and the abstract already
gestures at it ("real and temporary at once"). The danger is if it is used to *replace* the
cost-of-leaving story rather than *follow* from it. Fluidity without symmetric, named exit
costs is just churn dressed up; fluidity *with* them is autonomy. The paper opens on a durable
partnership that breaks — the reframe must not change the subject from that scene. The right
order is: state the ledger (points 1-3), then say the climate is what the ledger makes
possible. Fluidity is the consequence of safe exit, not an alternative to it.

As for the dissociation story I called half-answered: point 5 does not answer it, because it
says nothing about where the audience goes — it changes the frame from "loss" to "climate."
That is fine as framing; it must not be mistaken for resolution. And the paper should not
abandon the durable-partnership scene to make room for the ephemeral one. It should say the
protocol serves both, and that fluidity is the freedom to reshape, not a commitment to
ephemerality.

## The one sentence I'd add

In §1, immediately at "departure costs both sides," add:

> **"A writer who walks gives up the home of their byline; the publication that loses them
> loses the work itself — withdrawn from the record, no longer vouched for by the name — and
> when the leavers were the publication's voice, the name that remains is, to anyone who knew
> it, visibly a diminished thing."**

That is the publication's half of the ledger in one breath: the base case (the work leaves the
record and the vouching), the collective case (identity), and it is mechanism-grounded (the
record, the name) rather than sentimental. "The home of their byline" reuses the draft's own
vocabulary from §1. Keep the paper's existing clause about persuading and choosing after it,
so the ledger has both the loss *and* the mechanism.

Separately — because point 4 asked to be asked rather than dodged — if the authors want to
actually close the reader side rather than scope it out, the sentence that does it is: *"The
reader who came for a writer can follow the writer's account, not only the publication's
domain — the byline is the portable address, and the audience follows it or stays, case by
case, rather than belonging to the name by default."* If they are not prepared to build that,
they should say plainly that reader exit is out of scope and the protocol does not fix the
sock-puppet problem — with the same candor they used for the state. Either way, "case by case"
alone is not an answer.
