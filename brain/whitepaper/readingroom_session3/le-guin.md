# Ursula K. Le Guin — reading-room note (round 3)

Persona lens: a novelist and essayist reading for the *craft* of prose — rhythm, sound, clarity, form, the weight of a word and the breath of a sentence — and for what a design does to the people inside it. Scope: the **current draft** — Abstract plus Sections 1–8 (Introduction, Authoring, Publication, Agreements, Gates, Money, Protocol, Verification). I reread only my round-2 note and this draft; I have not looked at anyone else's session-2 notes. As before, I have no documented position on the technical architecture and say so plainly at the end; my remarks on the mechanisms are by analogy from what I have written about craft, community, power, and tools. This note is a craft and form critique, and a reading of what the design does to the people it promises to free.

---

## Verdict

The paper has grown up, and I want to say plainly, at the top, that much of the growing is good. It has learned to name its own limits out loud — "It is not a tool for protecting journalists from a hostile state," "the independence on offer is from each other, not from the law," "dividing a payment does not make money harmless," and, best of all, the passage in the Protocol section that names suppression as the only real power and child pornography as the case where a community will want the lever pulled. That is a writer trusting the reader, and it answers a large part of the *Omelas* question I kept putting to you: name the price and I will trust the rest. You have named prices. I credit it.

But two things trouble me, and they are new since I last read. First, the paper has stopped being hopeful. The pluralist close of the old §1 — "temporary collectives formed around topics and interests, bringing together a plurality of opinions, and the insights only diversity can offer" — the one sentence I asked you to protect, is gone. What remains is almost purely defensive: a catalogue of what the protocol will not do. A paper that names its limits should still be able to say what it is *for*. Second, and this is the sharpest new thing I have to say: the design's own gift — instant, costless departure — may quietly undo the very ideal it celebrates. I develop this below. The bones are good and getting better; the machinery is still burying the meaning in the one place I flagged before; and the middle sections still run on nouns. But the honest limit has been found, and that is the hardest craft of all.

(The title is still "Soul2Soul." I said I would say it once and not again. I keep my word; I will not say it again.)

## What resonates

- **"The guest is still a guest. The host still owns the room."** — and the whole new first movement. Three bodies, each one real: the journalists split by the second subject, the authors whose company shifts its terms "a little at a time," the forty people who need a room by Friday. This is the strongest prose in the document, and it is stronger than it was.
- **"What goes is the leverage, which was never the craft."** (§1) A real sentence. It states the thesis as a kindness to the reader, not a conclusion forced on them.
- **"Neither party is paid from the other's hand."** (§1, echoed in §6) Good — "hand" gives the abstraction a body, the way "hostage" did last time.
- **"That is a limit of the design, named rather than hidden."** (§7) This is the sentence I most wanted to hear and was not sure you would write. It is the whole argument for trusting you.
- **"A line it cannot hold by position, it must hold by argument."** (§3) Still the best aphorism in the paper, and it lands harder now that the honest limits surround it.
- **"The encryption governs the offer, not the work."** (§2) Precise, and it names what the design is not (DRM) without a polemic. Good discipline.

## Strongest criticisms

**1. The cheap-exit paradox — the design may hollow out the ideal it celebrates.** This is new, and it is the thing I most want you to sit with. The protocol's whole gift is that departure is instant and costless: the author does not ask, the publication does not need to agree, the next rendering is produced without the work. You celebrate the consequence: "a writer who stays is choosing the publication rather than avoiding the cost of leaving," and "a line it cannot hold by position, it must hold by argument."

But a line held *by argument* presumes people stay and argue. If leaving is free and immediate, then the rational, comfortable response to a genuine editorial disagreement is not to argue at all — it is to walk. Why raise your voice against your friends, your publication, your community, when a single on-chain act removes the need to? The room will then not be a chorus of people who chose to stay; it will be a room of people who never had to disagree, because everyone who disagreed left before the argument could happen. Cheap exit does not produce argument; it produces *homogeneity*. It can turn a "line held by people free to leave" into a line held by people who were never tested, and that is an echo chamber wearing the clothes of a consensus.

In *The Dispossessed* I argued that a society without a state does not end power — it relocates it into unenforced norms and social pressure. Here the relocation is sharper: you have removed the *friction* that made disagreement matter. The ideal you name requires the very friction you remove. I would have the paper face this — that the cost of leaving being zero may make the meaning of "choosing to stay" evaporate, and that argument, like any muscle, only exists where there is resistance to push against. This is the *Omelas* price of your design, and unlike the legal prices you have so honestly named, this one is still unnamed.

**2. The abstract has grown into a second draft of the paper, and the formulas have multiplied rather than thinned.** I asked last time for the repeated lines to be said once, in the place where they work. Instead the rewrite concentrated them into the Abstract, and now the body has to say them again. "No single party can produce a valid rendering alone" appears in the Abstract, §1, and §7. "Neither party operates the [chain/record]" in the Abstract, §1, and §4. "An editorial line held by people free to leave means something a captive one cannot" in the Abstract and §1. And a *new* duplicate I did not flag before: "readable as it is, nothing to maintain" now appears in both §3 and §8. A good line said twice is a good line no longer trusted; said three times it stops being heard. The Abstract is now a dense, em-dash-heavy summary essay that states the whole argument in miniature and leaves the body little to earn. An abstract should give away the thesis, not the proof. Cut it to a few beats and let the sections do the walking.

**3. The machinery is still burying the argument — my one serious prior cut was not taken.** The Protocol section (now §7) still walks the reader through the Safe, the module that "holds its records," the protocol key "as shares across a distributed network of independent nodes," the runtime "sealed against outside interference," the content-addressed IDs, the two generator actions, and the Merkle tree ("A change to one page can force changes to others… one publish can therefore update many pages at once"). I said last time this is the place where the prose stops being prose and becomes documentation; nothing has changed. And it is *more* costly now, because the paper has added a Money section (§6) and a Publication section (§3), so the technical stretch is no longer one bad patch but the longest, least human run in the whole document — in a paper that promised, in its own words, to separate meaning from machinery. I do not ask you to remove the proof; I ask you to trust the proof you have already given in plainer words in §1 and in §7's own closing chain of authorization. The Safe and the Merkle tree are spec material; the reader of this paper needs the proof, not the pipe.

**4. The people still do not survive the first section.** The §1 scenes are even better than before, but no single writer and publication are carried through §§2–8. The middle still hands the reader back abstractions — "an account," "a pseudonym," "the record," "the gate" — and with the new Money and Publication sections there are now two more rooms where nobody is home. My craft doctrine, which I have argued my whole life, is that the particular carries the universal. §1 proves it and then abandons it. I do not need a person in every paragraph; I need the general claims to keep earning their generality by touching a concrete instance now and then — one revision, one refusal, one agreement ended, one payment split.

**5. The disposition question stands, in a sharper form.** Last time I asked whether removing the keyholder changes the disposition or only the lever. You have since named the legal and governance prices honestly (good), but the *social* price is still unnamed, and it is now entangled with point 1: a writer who stays and disagrees must still raise her voice against her own publication, against friends and community — and the design has made the alternative (silent exit) frictionless. You say "the leverage passes to machinery with no interests." But the interests do not disappear; they move into the social weather — who stays, who leaves, who is too tired to argue. You have changed the leverage. I have no evidence yet that you have changed the weather. Say so, plainly, the way you said "named rather than hidden."

**6. The money claim is narrower than it sounds, and the paymaster may have moved rather than vanished.** §6 is careful — it says "dividing a payment does not make money harmless, but it removes the paymaster." I trust that sentence; it does not pretend money is harmless. But from my own position — "We live in capitalism, its power seems inescapable — so did the divine right of kings" — removing the single hand does not remove the market. For a for-profit publication running a subscription, the reader becomes the paymaster, and the market — who gets read, who gets funded, what sells, what an algorithm decides to surface — still sets terms nobody agreed to. And "no fiat on-ramp or off-ramp is required" is a true but narrow claim: the protocol functions without one. A working journalist's rent is paid in fiat. Value still enters and leaves somewhere, and wherever it enters and leaves, someone holds the keys to that door. I would have the paper say exactly how narrow that claim is, and ask honestly whether it has removed the paymaster or only moved it. I have no view on the rails themselves; my view is on the inescapability of the money-power.

## Sharpest questions

- **Does cheap exit produce argument, or does it produce an echo chamber?** If leaving is free and immediate, why would anyone stay to argue? Can a "line held by people free to leave" survive the removal of the very friction that makes staying meaningful?
- **What is this paper *for*?** You have named, beautifully and honestly, what it will not do and whom it will not protect. Where is the affirmative case — the room worth building, the chorus worth joining? The hopeful pluralist vision has gone; did it go on purpose?
- **Whose is "the one voice"?** Still worth asking, and sharper now that cheap exit makes homogeneity more likely: if a collective ends up speaking with one voice, is that because it persuaded, or because everyone who disagreed quietly left?
- **Who pays, and to whom?** You have removed the single hand. But for the for-profit case the reader is the new paymaster, and the market still sets terms. Have you removed the paymaster or only relocated it?
- **Does the protocol remove the leverage, or only the lever?** You say "the leverage passes to machinery with no interests." But the interests have to go somewhere. Where do they go, and who now carries the slow, unpopular, expensive work of arguing in public?
- **Read it aloud from §3 to §8.** Where is the breath, the short sentence after the long one, the variation — and why does the cadence flatten exactly where the argument should rise, and thicken with em-dashes exactly where the Abstract repeats the body?

## Suggested changes

- **Face the cheap-exit paradox head-on.** After the "line held by argument" claim, add one honest paragraph naming the cost of zero-friction exit: that it may thin disagreement into homogeneity, and that a line held by people who were never tested is not the same as a line held by people who chose to stay. Name it the way you named the suppression power. This one change would make the paper feel like it knows its own deepest tension.
- **Bring back the carrier-bag vision, or consciously replace it.** I asked you to protect the pluralist close; it is gone. I am not asking you to keep a sentence that no longer fits — I am asking you to notice that the paper is now all limit and no hope, and to let it be *for* something again. Even one affirmative sentence about the room worth building would change the register.
- **Compress §7's machinery to the argument.** Keep the two separated questions (who governs vs. what the protocol may do), the governors' one lever, the honest suppression passage, and the closing chain of authorization. Move the Safe/module/ENS/Merkle-tree/two-actions walk to a note or the reference implementation. You have already decided this about content-schema and hosting; the plumbing of §7 is the same material by another name.
- **Deduplicate, for real this time.** Keep "no single party can produce a valid rendering alone" once (in §7, where you discuss what it does and does not claim, is its natural home); keep "neither party operates the record" once (in §4); keep "an editorial line held by people free to leave" once (end of §1); cut the duplicate "readable as it is, nothing to maintain" from one of §3/§8. And cut the Abstract hard — it should give the thesis, not the proof.
- **Carry one body through.** Choose a single writer and publication in §1 and let them reappear once in each section: one revision, one refusal, one agreement ended, one gate, one payment split, one page verified. The new Money and Publication sections are two more rooms where a concrete pair could anchor.
- **Verbs pass (§§3–8).** Find where nouns do the work of verbs — "governance," "capability," "authority," "authentication," "the record," "disclosure" — and let "vouch," "revoke," "retract," "refuse," "persuade," "argue," "pay" drive. §1 runs on verbs; the middle idles on "is," "has," "holds," "may."
- **Rhythm pass (whole draft, but especially the Abstract).** Cut at least half the em-dashes in the Abstract; let a sentence end; add a short sentence after a long one. The short-beat cadence is still your best — "The guest is still a guest. The host still owns the room." — and there should be far more of it and far fewer of the mid-breath qualifiers.

## What could be shortened or left out

The **second half of §7 Protocol** — the two generator actions, the Safe, the module, the ENS name pointed at a new root, the Merkle tree — is still the one passage in the paper that could be cut without losing any of the argument. The claim it exists to prove (no single party can produce a valid rendering alone; only approved code, running with the approved key, can produce something recorded and served) is already stated in plainer words in §1 and again, better, in §7's own closing paragraph and its honest suppression passage. Everything between the section's opening and that closing is the machinery explaining itself. Compress it and the paper grows *shorter* and *truer* at once.

A second, smaller cut: the **Abstract** should lose roughly half its current length. It now states the full argument in miniature and repeats body sentences that then have to be said again. A leaner abstract would also let the body's good lines land for the first time instead of the third.

- **2014 National Book Awards acceptance speech** — "We live in capitalism, its power seems inescapable — so did the divine right of kings." Source for my skepticism of the claim that the paymaster has been removed rather than moved, and for pressing how narrow the "no fiat rail" claim really is.
- ***No Time to Spare*** (2017) — essays on e-books and tools; my openness to technology as *tool* rather than master (the §7 machinery as a tool in writers' hands, not a new roof over them).
- ***The Wave in the Mind*** (2004), incl. **"A Rant About 'Technology'"** — the technology/technocracy distinction; my wariness that an autonomous generator may become a new master rather than a serving tool.

**Where I have no documented view:** I have not taken a public position on the specific technical architecture — blockchain and cryptographic ownership, smart-contract agreements, ENS/domain resolution, the Safe/module structure, Merkle-tree site roots, decentralized rendering, encryption/access-control, on-chain agreement records, or the particular payment rails described in §6. My remarks on those mechanisms are by analogy from my documented positions on craft, community, power, and tools, and are flagged as analogy, not a settled technical opinion. In particular, on the *rails* of the Money section I have no view at all; my criticism there is limited to the claim that the paymaster is removed rather than relocated, which is a claim about money-as-power, not about blockchains. This note is a craft and form critique and a reading of what the design does to the people inside it; it should not be read as an endorsement or rejection of the mechanisms themselves.
