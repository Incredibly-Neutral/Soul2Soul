# Polyphony
Writers conform less because anyone tells them to than because dissent is expensive: there are few publications to move to, and moving means abandoning the work. This paper proposes a protocol that lowers both costs. It takes the press, which turns a manuscript into a page, out of the publication's hands and runs it as open code on sealed hardware that neither author nor publication controls. First, starting a publication takes little effort. Second, an author can end an agreement alone, at once, on a public ledger; the pages are rebuilt without the work, and no one can stall that. Finally, every page shows who wrote it, that both sides consented, and that it is unchanged. What remains is trust in that hardware, and in its owners not to collude. A publication keeps its writers only while they choose to stay
